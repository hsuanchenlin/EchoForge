# EchoForge 0.9.3

**Three user-visible changes: ⌥A now starts listening on the press instead of
putting an idle card on screen, a YouTube channel Kongweh names back to you is
written `@name`, and the History list is a designed, responsive card that no
longer wastes the width you drag the window out to.** Underneath them, the
microphone is now owned by whichever press took it, so a dictation and an Ask
can no longer end each other's recording. Nothing about recognition,
transcripts, styles, storage or the security model changes in this build, and
your recordings, personal terms, snippets, styles, shortcuts and downloaded
models are untouched by this update.

> The download is still `EchoForge.dmg` and it still installs `EchoForge.app`.
> The app introduces itself as Kongweh once it starts. That split is deliberate -
> see "One app, two names" in the 0.9.1 notes.

## ⌥A records

**Press ⌥A and Kongweh starts listening.** The panel opens *and* the microphone
opens, the way the dictation key, ⌥S and the ⌥Y channel command all already
worked. Speak your question, **press ⌥A again to finish it**, and the answer is
drawn on the card.

For one release it did neither. ⌥A opened the panel and stopped there, leaving
an idle card with an **Ask by voice** button you had to find with the mouse -
which is not what a press-to-record shortcut is for. It also frequently appeared
without taking the keyboard, so Escape and everything else you typed went to the
app behind it; the panel's activation is now forced, the way the channel
picker's already was.

Two consequences of making the key record are worth knowing, because they change
what a press does:

- **It is no longer a show/hide toggle.** A key that closed an open panel could
  not also be a key that always starts listening. So a second press *finishes
  the question you are asking*, and the panel is closed with **Esc** or the
  **Close** button - and by nothing else. **Discard recording** gives the
  microphone back and puts the card back to the answer it was showing; it closes
  nothing.
- **A press that arrives while something else is recording is ignored**, rather
  than interrupting it. In particular a ⌥A pressed mid-dictation does nothing at
  all: bringing this app to the front there would send that dictation's paste
  into the question field.

**A question that fails now stays on the card beside the failure.** A spoken
question exists nowhere else - you never typed it, and nothing stores it - so a
bare error sentence left you unable to tell a misheard question from a model
that could not run. You can now read what Kongweh thought you asked.

Your question and its answer stay **paired in the panel's conversation** while
it is open, so a follow-up question is asked with the exchange before it in
view. That conversation lives only as long as the panel: closing it clears the
lot, and a failed question never enters it. As before, ⌥A runs the same
on-device Apple system model the rewriting stage does; **there is no cloud path
for the Ask panel at all**, in this build or any other, and that is enforced in
code rather than by a setting.

The recording itself is kept in History the way it always was, labelled **Ask**.

### Your dictation and your question can no longer take the microphone from each other

Four keys reach one microphone - the two dictation keys, ⌥A and ⌥S - and until
now a press could quietly take it from a capture that was already running.
Stopping the Ask panel could return a *dictation's* audio and paste a spoken
question into the document you were typing in, and the main window's record
button could end a capture it never started.

A recording is now **owned**: one press takes the microphone, and stopping or
cancelling names the session it means, so a caller that no longer holds it is
refused instead of obeyed. That is the whole of it, and it is why a press can
now be turned down. Nothing about what is recorded, where it is stored or what
leaves the Mac changes - this is isolation between the app's own four
shortcuts.

## A channel is named back to you as `@name`

Where Kongweh **names a channel to you**, it now writes it `@name`: the picker's
rows and what VoiceOver reads out for them, the sentence naming the channels an
ambiguous phrase collided on, the History row that collision writes, and the
report saying which channel a video was opened from.

That is presentation and nothing else. **This app still never resolves a
handle**, never searches YouTube, and reaches a channel only by the canonical
`UC…` id you pasted into Settings yourself. So the `@` is put in front of *your
own label*, verbatim, and nothing is folded away to make it look more like a
real handle - a channel you stored as `valley 101` is shown as `@valley 101`,
not `@valley101`, because the tidier spelling would be an identity the app
invented for a channel it reaches by number.

Anything quoting **what to say** stays exactly as it was: the phrase that was
heard at the top of the picker, the "Also said as …" spelling under a row, the
Settings list of your channels and its usage example. That list is the reference
for what to say out loud, and teaching you to say "at Veritasium" would be worse
than the inconsistency.

The picker's filter box is the one place a handle comes back *in*: its rows now
say `@Veritasium`, so a single leading `@` is dropped from what you type before
it is compared - otherwise typing what the list in front of you says would
report that none of your channels match.

**Spoken matching is untouched.** A name you say is still compared against the
spellings you stored, through the same two deterministic tiers, with ambiguity
detected and refused in each tier separately. The allowlist is still the whole
security model, and nothing here widens what one press can reach.

## The History list is a card now

The list of recordings was one fixed layout at one width. Dragged wider, the
metadata stayed stacked in a column with several hundred points of empty card
beside it; the transcript could truncate to a single line while claiming three;
and the transcript sat indented past the badge and the timestamp above it on
every row, at every width.

Each row is now **a designed card with two layouts and one threshold**. Below
about 480 pt the metadata stacks under the label; above it, the relative time
and the duration sit inline as chips beside it and the footer shows the full
date rather than only the clock time. Nothing truncates that would tell you
nothing truncated - a header that would collide at some width or text size
stacks instead.

The rest of the polish:

- **the relative time keeps up**. "2 minutes ago" now becomes "3 minutes ago"
  while you are looking at it, rather than only when the row is redrawn - and
  VoiceOver is read the same timestamp the card is showing, not a different one;
- **a queued or running row shows a progress ring**, determinate where there is
  a fraction to show and spinning where there is not, in place of the static
  clock glyph it used to sit under;
- **a failed row is marked in words as well as colour** - "Transcription failed"
  beside a warm card border, because a colour alone says nothing to somebody who
  cannot see it;
- **VoiceOver gets the same actions the pointer does.** Which actions a row
  offers is a single decision read twice, once for the hover bar and once for
  VoiceOver, which has no pointer to hover with. A queued row keeps **delete**,
  which is the way out; a failed row keeps **regenerate**, which is the way
  forward.

Dark mode is designed for its own ground rather than being the light card dimmed.
[docs/history-card.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/history-card.md)
is the whole story.

## Everything else is unchanged from 0.9.2

- **Every shortcut is where it was.** ⌥` records, ⌥A opens Ask *and listens*,
  ⌥S asks about the screen, ⌥M switches engine, ⌥Y runs the YouTube command -
  all rebindable in **Settings → Shortcuts**.
- **The ⌥Y channel picker is unchanged.** A misheard name still offers your own
  channels, ordered with the closest spelling first, and still opens nothing
  until you press Return. Turning off "Let me pick from my channels when a name
  is not recognised" in **Settings → Dictionary & Snippets → YouTube Channels**
  still restores the previous refusal exactly.
  [docs/youtube-latest-video.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/youtube-latest-video.md).
- **History still says what each entry was** - Dictation, Ask, File
  transcription, YouTube command - and entries from before 0.9.1 are still
  labelled "Older recording" and never relabelled. The sentence under a label is
  still written for you and holds no channel ID, feed address, video URL or
  anything from your Keychain.
  [docs/history-provenance.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/history-provenance.md).
- **Chinese transcripts are still written in one script**, Traditional unless
  you picked Simplified, as an output setting only: it never makes recognition
  Chinese, never moves your dictation language and never touches Japanese or
  Korean.
  [docs/chinese-script.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/chinese-script.md).
- **The cloud path is still opt-in and still off.** Two features can be pointed
  at an OpenAI-compatible provider, speech transcription and the spoken
  "Translate to …" command, and switching either takes an explicit acceptance of
  a sheet naming what is uploaded and where. Style rewriting, the Ask panel
  (⌥A), screen questions (⌥S) and channel-name matching have no cloud path at
  all, enforced in code. A 0.9.3 install you never point at a provider sends
  nothing, anywhere, with the single exception of the YouTube command if you add
  a channel and use it: that request carries a channel id you typed and nothing
  about you.
  [docs/cloud-api.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/cloud-api.md).
  If you need "not present" rather than "not enabled",
  `Scripts/build_release.sh --offline-only` still builds exactly that; it is not
  published as a separate download here.
- **The app is still thin.** It ships no model weights. The model you choose is
  fetched on demand and checksummed, SenseVoiceSmall as a verified model pack
  and every other engine by its own downloader, and lands in Application
  Support, where it survives replacing the app. This build does not change the
  SenseVoiceSmall pack, so an engine you have already downloaded is not fetched
  again.
  [docs/model-packs.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/model-packs.md).
- **Kongweh still never checks for updates on its own.** Checking, downloading
  and installing remain three separate things you press.

## Installing this build

This release is **not signed with an Apple Developer ID and not notarized**, so
macOS will refuse to open it on the first try, typically "the app is damaged and
can't be opened", or "Apple could not verify EchoForge is free of malware".
Nothing is wrong with the download; macOS simply cannot check who built it.

1. Open `EchoForge.dmg` and drag `EchoForge.app` into `Applications`.
   Do not run it from the mounted disk image.
2. Then do **one** of the following, once:
   - Right-click (or Control-click) the app in `Applications`, choose **Open**,
     and click **Open** in the dialog; or
   - run `xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/EchoForge.app` in Terminal
     (adjust the path if you installed it elsewhere).

The app in Finder is still `EchoForge.app`; it introduces itself as Kongweh once
it starts.

### Expect to grant permissions again

Because these builds are unsigned, macOS treats each one as a **new
application** for privacy purposes. After you replace your copy, it will ask
again for **Microphone** and **Accessibility**, and for **Screen Recording** the
first time you use ⌥S. **This is expected and is not a regression**; it happens
on every unsigned update and will keep happening until these builds are signed
with a Developer ID.

If a stale **EchoForge** entry is already listed under **System Settings →
Privacy & Security → Accessibility**, remove it with the minus button and add
the new app back; macOS otherwise keeps showing the old entry as granted while
the new build has nothing. The new entry may appear under either name.

The same reason produces one more prompt, and only for people who have stored a
cloud API key: the first time the updated app reads it, macOS asks whether it
may use information stored in your keychain. Choose **Always Allow** and it
stops asking. An install that has never turned the cloud path on never reads the
Keychain at all.

Replacing an existing install keeps your recordings, personal terms, snippets,
styles and downloaded models.

Full instructions, including how to use the Chinese engines and the basics of
recording:
[docs/install.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/install.md).

## How this build was made

Produced by `Scripts/build_release.sh` and published only after
`Scripts/verify_release_package.sh` checked every embedded library's signature
and `@rpath` dependency and **started the app**.
