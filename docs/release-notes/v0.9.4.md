# EchoForge 0.9.4

**Two new things you can do with the words that are already there, and two
things that stop getting in your way.** ⌥E edits text you have highlighted in
any app: speak an instruction and the selection is replaced. The ✨ button on a
history card fixes what a recording misheard. Light-mode history cards no longer
wash out under the pointer. And an update download keeps going when you switch
Settings tabs, rather than starting over. Nothing about recognition, storage or
the security model changes in this build, and your recordings, personal terms,
snippets, styles, shortcuts and downloaded models are untouched by this update.

> The download is still `EchoForge.dmg` and it still installs `EchoForge.app`.
> The app introduces itself as Kongweh once it starts. That split is deliberate -
> see "One app, two names" in the 0.9.1 notes.

## Voice edit: ⌥E rewrites what you have highlighted

Highlight text in any application, **press ⌥E**, speak what you want done to it,
and the selection is replaced with the result:

- *"make it concise bullet points"*
- *"translate to Traditional Chinese"*
- *"fix the grammar"*
- *"format as a Swift model"*

**If nothing is highlighted, your clipboard is edited instead**, so a paragraph
you copied out of somewhere can be rewritten without pasting it anywhere first.
The card tells you which one is happening - **Editing Selection...** or
**Editing Clipboard...** - so you are never guessing which text a press is
about. A press that finds no text at all never takes the microphone; it says
**Nothing to edit** and stops.

**⌥E is its own key, and that is the whole design.** A dictation is words on
their way *into* your document; a voice edit is an instruction *about* words
already in it. Sharing the dictation key would make every dictation ambiguous -
"make it concise bullet points" would have to be guessed as either those five
words or an instruction about whatever happened to be selected. With two keys
Kongweh never has to guess, which is the same reason ⌥Y has its own key. **The
instruction you speak is never pasted**; only the rewrite of the captured text
is.

The key is rebindable in **Settings → Shortcuts → Voice Edit**, beside the
dictation, Ask and screen-question keys, and it shares their press-and-hold
behaviour and their microphone ownership - a press while another key is
listening is refused rather than seizing the recording.

### Your text is left alone unless the edit actually worked

Three rules make a press safe to try on real writing:

- **A rewrite is only ever pasted back into the selection it was made from.**
  The seconds a model takes are seconds in which you can click somewhere else,
  and a paste that lands in the next field along would replace text nobody asked
  about. So before ⌘V, Kongweh checks that it is the same app, the same window,
  the same focused element and the same selected range, *and* that the selection
  still reads exactly as the text that was captured. Anything else pastes
  nothing and says **Target app unavailable**.
- **A refused, timed-out or unavailable rewrite changes nothing.** The same
  `StyleRewriteGuard` that protects dictation rewriting is the boundary here: an
  empty answer, a model talking to you instead of editing, or a number or
  currency symbol that was not in your text is refused, and the selection is
  left as it was. A style may omit; nothing may invent. The same happens when
  the model hands your text back unchanged - pasting the original over itself
  would replace a rich-text selection with a plain-string copy.
- **It is on-device only**, with no cloud path at all, enforced in code rather
  than by a setting. The selected text is whatever you had on screen, and a
  press that quietly posted it to a provider is not a trade this app makes. It
  needs macOS 26 with Apple Intelligence, the same as the other model features.

Capture tries three sources in order and the first usable one wins: the
Accessibility selected text of the focused element (which touches no clipboard
at all), then a simulated ⌘C **with your pasteboard restored afterwards**, then
the clipboard as it already stood.

### It is in History with a full before and after

The row is labelled **Voice edit**, and the sentence under the label is the
instruction you spoke. The highlighted text is kept as the original and the
rewrite as the new text, so **Compare** and **Show original** - the buttons that
were already there - show you exactly what changed. The audio is the
instruction.

**The row is written before your selection is touched.** Until it exists, your
selection is the only copy of the original text, so a row that cannot be saved
is a voice edit that does not happen: nothing is pasted, nothing is lost, and
the card says **Could not save edit**.

[docs/selection-edit.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/selection-edit.md)
is the whole story.

## ✨ Fix with AI, on any history card

Press the **✨** on a recording card and the transcript is read back to the
on-device model, which is asked to fix the recognition errors that deterministic
post-processing cannot infer: **Chinese homophones** - 「我**再**開會」 for
「我**在**開會」 - mis-heard words, and word boundaries split in the wrong
place. These are exactly the mistakes that need the sentence around a word to
spot, which is why a dictionary cannot catch them and a model can.

Four things about it are worth knowing:

- **It can only correct a transcript or leave it alone.** Every path out returns
  the row unchanged or a correction that survived the guard, so a press costs
  you a wait and nothing else. The audio file is never touched, no recording is
  deleted, and no row but the one you pressed is written.
- **The original is always one click away.** What the card said before the press
  becomes its original text, so **Show original** and **Compare** read it the
  way they always have, and a corrected row is marked **AI Polished** beside its
  timestamp. A row that post-processing had already changed keeps the *engine's*
  own words as that copy - the only record of what was actually said - so a
  second press cannot overwrite it. Regenerating a row clears the mark, because
  the words are the engine's again.
- **Your personal terms are protected.** Every entry in your dictionary whose
  spelling appears in the transcript has to survive the correction. Those are
  spellings you typed yourself to say how your own vocabulary is written, and a
  model asked to fix homophones is precisely the thing that would "correct" a
  name back into one.
- **It is on-device only** and has no cloud path, enforced in code. A history
  row is every dictation you ever made.

You do not need dictation-time rewriting switched on to use it - this is a
button you pressed, not a stage running behind you. A failure never interrupts
you with an alert: it says why on the card, and every word the row had is still
there.

[docs/history-ai-fix.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/history-ai-fix.md)
is the whole story.

## History cards no longer wash out under the pointer

In light mode the window, the card and the hover fill were all white or nearly
so, which meant **hovering a history row made it disappear into the window**
instead of lifting off it.

The canvas behind the list is now a grouped gray and **the cards stay white**,
including under the pointer: the hover is a firmer stroke and a raised shadow
rather than a fill change that has nothing to contrast against. The lift is
deliberately small - a card is a container for text you are reading, and a hover
strong enough to catch out of the corner of your eye is a hover that makes the
words harder to read.

The intended dark palette is unchanged and still designed for its own ground
rather than being the light card dimmed: no dark colour was re-picked, and a
hover still lifts the card a shade instead of changing its stroke and shadow.
A bug in how that hover colour was computed is fixed: it could be resolved
against the current appearance instead of the dark card, making a hovered card
come out near-white rather than showing the intended subtle lift. It now always
resolves against the dark card, avoiding that flash.

## An update download survives switching Settings tabs

Starting a download in **Settings → About** and then looking at another tab used
to **destroy the transfer**. Settings builds only the tab that is showing, and
the download lived inside the About pane, so switching away took it with it: the
progress stopped being reported, a bundle part-way through verification was
abandoned, and coming back to About offered to start the whole 222 MB download
again from zero.

The download now **belongs to the app rather than to the pane it started in**.
It keeps going while you read another tab and while Settings is closed
altogether, and returning to About shows the transfer that is actually running.
A tab switch resets nothing - a ready-to-install update and a live offer both
survive one - and the pane only clears a finished "you are up to date" or a
previous failure when you reopen Settings, because those were answers to a
question you have stopped asking.

Quitting mid-download is safe and always was: **what has already arrived is
kept**, so the next attempt continues from where it stopped rather than starting
over. Quitting during the verify-and-install step now stands the disk-image
commands down in the right order before detaching, so a quit at that exact
moment cannot leave a mount behind. A staged update that is ready to install is
never discarded on the way out.

As before, **Kongweh never checks for updates on its own**: checking,
downloading and installing are three separate things you press, and an update is
only ever downloaded from this repository's own GitHub releases.

## Everything else is unchanged from 0.9.3

- **Every other shortcut is where it was.** ⌥` records, ⌥A opens Ask *and
  listens*, ⌥S asks about the screen, ⌥M switches engine, ⌥Y runs the YouTube
  command - all rebindable in **Settings → Shortcuts**, now with ⌥E beside them.
- **The microphone is still owned by whichever press took it**, so a dictation,
  an Ask, a screen question and a voice edit cannot end one another's recording.
  A press that arrives while something else is listening is refused rather than
  interrupting it.
- **History still says what each entry was** - Dictation, Ask, Voice edit, File
  transcription, YouTube command - and entries from before 0.9.1 are still
  labelled "Older recording" and never relabelled. The sentence under a label is
  written for you and holds no channel ID, feed address, video URL or anything
  from your Keychain.
  [docs/history-provenance.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/history-provenance.md).
- **Chinese transcripts are still written in one script**, Traditional unless
  you picked Simplified, as an output setting only: it never makes recognition
  Chinese, never moves your dictation language and never touches Japanese or
  Korean.
  [docs/chinese-script.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/chinese-script.md).
- **The cloud path is still opt-in and still off.** Two features can be pointed
  at an OpenAI-compatible provider, speech transcription and the spoken
  "Translate to …" command, and switching either takes an explicit acceptance of
  a sheet naming what is uploaded and where. Style rewriting, Fix with AI, voice
  edit (⌥E), the Ask panel (⌥A), screen questions (⌥S) and channel-name matching
  have no cloud path at all, enforced in code. A 0.9.4 install you never point
  at a provider sends nothing, anywhere, with the single exception of the
  YouTube command if you add a channel and use it: that request carries a
  channel id you typed and nothing about you.
  [docs/cloud-api.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/cloud-api.md).
  If you need "not present" rather than "not enabled",
  `Scripts/build_release.sh --offline-only` still builds exactly that; it is not
  published as a separate download here.
- **The ⌥Y channel picker is unchanged.** A misheard name still offers your own
  channels, closest spelling first, and still opens nothing until you press
  Return.
  [docs/youtube-latest-video.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/youtube-latest-video.md).
- **The app is still thin.** It ships no model weights. The model you choose is
  fetched on demand and checksummed, SenseVoiceSmall as a verified model pack
  and every other engine by its own downloader, and lands in Application
  Support, where it survives replacing the app. This build does not change the
  SenseVoiceSmall pack, so an engine you have already downloaded is not fetched
  again.
  [docs/model-packs.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/model-packs.md).

## Installing this build

This release is **not signed with an Apple Developer ID and not notarized**, so
macOS will refuse to open it on the first try, typically "the app is damaged and
can't be opened", or "Apple could not verify EchoForge is free of malware".
Nothing is wrong with the download; macOS simply cannot check who built it.

1. Open `EchoForge.dmg` and drag `EchoForge.app` into `Applications`.
   Do not run it from the mounted disk image.
2. Then do **one** of the following, once:
   - Right-click (or Control-click) the app in `Applications`, choose **Open**,
     and click **Open** in the dialog; or
   - run `xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/EchoForge.app` in Terminal
     (adjust the path if you installed it elsewhere).

The app in Finder is still `EchoForge.app`; it introduces itself as Kongweh once
it starts.

### Expect to grant permissions again

Because these builds are unsigned, macOS treats each one as a **new
application** for privacy purposes. After you replace your copy, it will ask
again for **Microphone** and **Accessibility**, and for **Screen Recording** the
first time you use ⌥S. **This is expected and is not a regression**; it happens
on every unsigned update and will keep happening until these builds are signed
with a Developer ID.

Voice edit reads the selected text through **Accessibility**, the same grant
dictation already needs to paste, so there is no new permission to give for ⌥E.

If a stale **EchoForge** entry is already listed under **System Settings →
Privacy & Security → Accessibility**, remove it with the minus button and add
the new app back; macOS otherwise keeps showing the old entry as granted while
the new build has nothing. The new entry may appear under either name.

The same reason produces one more prompt, and only for people who have stored a
cloud API key: the first time the updated app reads it, macOS asks whether it
may use information stored in your keychain. Choose **Always Allow** and it
stops asking. An install that has never turned the cloud path on never reads the
Keychain at all.

Replacing an existing install keeps your recordings, personal terms, snippets,
styles and downloaded models.

Full instructions, including how to use the Chinese engines and the basics of
recording:
[docs/install.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/install.md).
