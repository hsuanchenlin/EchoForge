# EchoForge 0.9.6

**The app stops being something you have to already understand.** Release after
release has added engines, styles, shortcuts and panes, and every one of them
assumed you knew which of those pieces you had. This build answers that: a Setup
tab that says in seven lines whether the next press of a key will produce text, a
menu bar that says what the app is doing without opening a window, an inventory
of every model with what it costs and what is safe to delete, and a microphone
that tells you it is not working *while* you are talking rather than through a
transcript that came back wrong.

Two things are also new about the dictation itself. You can **speak a
correction** - "send it Friday, scratch that, Monday" - and have it carried out,
deterministically and on this Mac. And the app you are dictating **into** can
prime the recognizer with words that suit it, so `viewDidLoad` in Xcode and "Best
regards" in Mail stop coming back as the nearest ordinary English.

There is also a **command-line tool**, `echoforge`, and a Homebrew formula to
install it.

Both new dictation features are **off by default** and your recordings, personal
terms, snippets, styles, shortcuts and downloaded models are untouched by this
update.

> The download is still `EchoForge.dmg` and it still installs `EchoForge.app`.
> The app introduces itself as Kongweh once it starts. That split is deliberate -
> see "One app, two names" in the 0.9.1 notes.

## Say "scratch that" and have it happen

**Settings → Dictionary & Snippets → Spoken Corrections**, off by default.

People do not dictate finished sentences. They say *"send it Friday, scratch
that, Monday"*, they say *"replace Friday with Monday"*, and then they go and
edit it by hand. Kongweh now reads those retractions off the transcript and
carries them out before anything is pasted:

| Said | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| `Send it Friday, scratch that, Monday.` | `Monday.` |
| `Replace Friday with Monday.` | the last `Friday` already said becomes `Monday` |
| `Delete the last sentence.` | everything back to the last full stop |
| `Start over.` | everything before it |

The Chinese forms are there too - `說錯了`, `講錯了`, `算了`, `刪掉上一句`,
`把星期五改成星期一`, `重新開始` - in whichever script you write in. The whole
table is printed in Settings, verbatim, so you can read the exact phrases before
you switch it on.

**This is the one stage in the app that deletes words**, into an application you
are not looking at, after the audio has stopped. So every rule in it is written
towards refusing:

- **A trigger only fires when it is the whole of a clause.** That single rule is
  what keeps *"I want to scratch that itch"*, *"could you delete that when you
  have a moment?"* and *"we should start over on the design, but not today"* as
  ordinary dictation. It has a documented cost: an engine that returns no
  punctuation at all produces one clause, so no trigger can fire on it. Paraformer
  is that engine.
- **An edit it cannot place is not made.** `scratch that` with nothing in front
  of it, or `replace Friday with Monday` when Friday was never said, leaves your
  words exactly as they arrived. Deciding on your behalf what you must have meant
  is the one thing this stage may not do: a missed correction costs a retry, a
  wrong one costs a sentence.
- **Everything it did not touch comes back byte for byte.** A dictation
  containing no correction is returned character for character unchanged.
- **It runs on live dictation only.** A dropped file, a re-transcription from
  History, `echoforge transcribe`, a ⌥E voice-edit instruction and a ⌥Y channel
  name are all refused, each for its own reason - "scratch that" in an interview
  recording is a thing the speaker said, and *"replace Friday with Monday"* spoken
  at ⌥E **is** the instruction.

A second, smaller toggle underneath prunes **hesitation sounds**: `um`, `uh`,
`er`, `erm` and their spellings are removed anywhere as whole words, and `like`,
`you know`, `I mean`, `sort of`, `kind of` only when the pause made them a clause
of their own - *"it's, like, complicated"* is a tic and *"I like this"* is a
sentence. Nothing inside quotation marks or backticks is ever touched, because a
transcript that says ``the token is `uh` in the parser`` is quoting the sound
rather than making it. There are deliberately **no Chinese fillers in the list**:
嗯, 那個 and 就是 are ordinary words far more often than they are noise.

**The engine's own words are never what gets lost.** History's "Show original"
still shows exactly what was said, Compare diffs the two, and
`echoforge history --json` carries it as `originalTranscript`. The corrected text
is what was pasted; the raw one is what is kept.
[docs/spoken-corrections.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/spoken-corrections.md)
is the whole story.

## The app you are dictating into can prime the recognizer

**Settings → Dictionary & Snippets → App Vocabulary**, off by default.

Dictating into Xcode and dictating into Mail are not the same activity. One wants
`viewDidLoad` and `snake_case`; the other wants "Best regards" and an email
address. Four built-in profiles now add words and a short passage to what Whisper
is primed with, chosen by the app in front of you:

| Kind of app | What it primes |
| --- | --- |
| Code & terminals | Identifier casing, language and tooling words |
| Mail | Sign-offs, an email address, mail vocabulary |
| Chat & messaging | Short-form work chat, the punctuation people actually use |
| Documents & notes | Headings, list markers, `[[wikilinks]]`, note vocabulary |

**Every profile is printed in the pane, in full** - the passage and the word
list - because being asked to let an app prime your recognizer and not being
shown with what is not a reasonable thing to ask.

Four rules bound it:

- **The only signal read is the frontmost app's bundle identifier.** Not the
  window title, not the document name, not the web address, not the field the
  caret is in, and nothing is logged, stored or sent anywhere. That is the same
  invariant app-aware style has always had, enforced the same way, with a source
  scan over the code that would need to reach further.
- **Web browsers deliberately get nothing.** A browser window is a mail client, a
  chat client, a code review and a text editor on four tabs, and the only thing
  that would tell them apart is the page address - which this feature refuses to
  look at.
- **Your own words always win.** Everything is composed into one prompt against
  one token budget, in priority order: your typed Initial Prompt, then your
  personal terms, then the app's passage and its words. A prompt that runs out of
  room drops the app's contribution and never one of yours.
- **It reaches Whisper and no other engine, and never the cloud.** Parakeet,
  SenseVoice and Paraformer take no decoding prompt and no stand-in is invented
  for them; while one of those is selected the pane says so rather than implying
  the setting is doing something. The cloud provider *does* take a prompt and is
  still sent your typed setting alone.

It can be switched off wholesale, for a kind of app, or for one named app that
beats its category.
[docs/app-vocabulary.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/app-vocabulary.md).

## Watch the words arrive

While Whisper decodes, the capsule now shows **what the engine has already
committed**, growing along the bottom of the pill.

The word that matters is committed. whisper.cpp emits a segment once it has
decided what that segment says and never revises one, so the line only ever
grows: it is not a flickering hypothesis being corrected in front of you, which
is the whole reason the app is willing to show it at all. A cross-engine
*streaming* contract, with unstable text and revisions, is a much larger promise
and this is not it - the other three local engines return one final string, so
they show the spinner alone exactly as before.

The line shows the **tail**, because a decode that has been running for thirty
seconds has already said the beginning and what you want to know is where it has
got to. VoiceOver reads it as "Transcribed so far", so a screen reader does not
announce a growing transcript as though the dictation had finished.

## A microphone that is not working now says so

A level meter confirms that *something* arrived. It could not tell you that the
wrong input was selected, that a headset came up on its call profile, or that you
were too far from the machine - three of the ways a dictation quietly comes back
wrong, all of which you find out about from the transcript.

The capsule now names the state and the input device beside the meter:

| State | Raised when |
| --- | --- |
| **No signal** | nothing above -42 dBFS has arrived at any point in the capture |
| **Low signal** | the loudest peak of the whole capture is under -28 dBFS |
| **Clipping** | a buffer peaked at or above -1 dBFS |
| *(nothing)* | ordinary speech, and the first 1.5 s of every capture |

Both halves of the measurement are new: the mean answers "is anything arriving",
and only the **peak** answers "is this being clipped" - a voice peaking at 0 dBFS
between syllables averages out around -18 dBFS, which reads as a healthy
recording right up until the transcript comes back full of crushed consonants.

Four rules keep it from becoming noise. **Nothing is claimed in the first 1.5
seconds**, which is when a Bluetooth input is still reaching gain and you have not
started talking - clipping is the only exception, because it is the one state
already damaging the recording. **The verdict is about the whole capture**, so
somebody who says a sentence and then thinks is never told their microphone went
silent. **A merely quiet recording is not painted as a warning**, and colour never
carries a diagnostic on its own - the line says the same thing in words. And
**nothing is refused**: you may be deliberately whispering, and a recording the
app declined because it disagreed about the volume would be worse than a quiet
transcript.

The line names the input device, because "no signal" is not actionable until you
know which microphone the app is on - and you are dictating into another
application and cannot go and look. It is not clickable, because the HUD ignores
mouse events for the whole recording rather than taking the top strip of the
screen away from the app you are typing in. Acting on it lives in the new Setup
tab, which has a microphone test beside it.
[docs/capsule-hud.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/capsule-hud.md).

## One pane that answers "is Kongweh ready?"

**Settings → Setup**, the new first tab.

The other eight panes are organised by the part of the app they configure, which
is the right shape for changing a setting and the wrong shape for finding out
whether the settings you already have add up to a working dictation. You had to
understand engines, models, languages, permissions, shortcuts and the cloud
boundary before you could tell whether the next press of a key would produce
text.

Setup is that answer in seven lines:

- **Transcription engine** - the one you chose, the one actually running, and why
  they differ.
- **Model** - ready, downloading, half-installed, and what it costs on disk.
- **Language** - the dictation language, and the Chinese output script where it
  applies.
- **Microphone** - the input and its permission.
- **Permissions** - Accessibility, which is required, and Screen Recording, which
  is only needed if you use ⌥S.
- **Dictation shortcut** - the trigger *actually in force*, and collisions
  between Kongweh's own shortcuts.
- **Where your speech goes** - the whole cloud position in one line.

Every row links to the tab that owns the fix, and **nothing on the pane writes
anything**: it does not download a model, select an engine or grant a permission.
Describing a state is not the same act as changing it, which is the separation
the app already keeps between the engine you chose and the engine that can run
now.

Two smaller decisions are worth knowing. **Rows never reorder** - a pane that
sorts itself worst-first moves under you every time something changes, so the
one-line summary above them carries the urgency instead ("Kongweh is ready",
"…with a few things worth knowing", "Kongweh cannot dictate yet"). And the
shortcut row reports collisions **between this app's own shortcuts only**: macOS
exposes no supported way to enumerate another application's global hotkeys, so a
check claiming conflicts in general would be claiming what it cannot know.

### The five-second microphone test

The one thing on the pane that *does* something, and the only place in the app
where you can find out whether the input you are on actually hears you - every
other microphone answer arrives during a dictation, when you are looking at
another application.

**The audio is thrown away, always.** The test ends by cancelling, which deletes
the file, so it leaves no `.wav`, no history row and nothing for the retention
policy to reason about. It takes the same single microphone claim everything else
does, so it is refused while a dictation is in flight and cannot start a capture
behind one. And the verdict is the shipped one - the same thresholds the capsule
uses - rather than a second, kinder opinion. Stopping it early says it was too
short to tell, instead of reporting a working input as "No signal".
[docs/setup-health.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/setup-health.md).

## The menu bar became a control centre

The old menu opened the app, chose a language, chose a microphone and quit. None
of those answer the questions you have *between* dictations, and all of them were
only visible by opening the main window - which for a menu-bar-only install means
bringing the whole app forward.

The menu now leads with **what the app is doing** (Ready, Recording,
Transcribing, a model preparing with its percentage, a permission missing, no
engine, shortcuts paused), then:

- **Start / Stop Dictation**, badged with the trigger actually in force;
- **Pause Kongweh Shortcuts**;
- **Engine, Language and Microphone**, each showing the current value on the
  parent item;
- **the last three transcripts**, with Copy and Open in History;
- History, Settings, About and Quit.

**Pause means the shortcuts and never the microphone.** A dictation app that
silently muted the machine would be doing something to a resource it does not
own, and you would find out in your next meeting. What it does is stand the
triggers down - the six global shortcuts are unregistered and the modifier-key
and mouse-button monitors stopped, so while it is paused ⌥` types a backtick
again. It is **not persisted**, because an app that came back paused after a
relaunch looks broken; Esc still cancels a session already in flight.

The recent-transcript rows only offer what is worth copying: finished, with
words, and not a spoken command or a question whose answer lives somewhere else.
Copy puts the **whole** transcript on the pasteboard, not the line that was shown.

### The bear is gone

The status item used to be a leftover upstream bear silhouette that matched
neither the old EchoForge icon nor the current one. It is now the same **Speech
Ripple** mark as the app icon, drawn to the same rules, in three silhouettes: the
mark, the mark with a record dot, and the mark with two pause bars. It never
animates - a menu-bar icon that moves is one that is noticed all day, and the app
has nothing to say continuously. A model downloading in the background
deliberately keeps the ordinary icon, because dictation carries on.

One bug found by driving the real status item is fixed here: on an unsigned build
the menu could **fail to open at all**, taking the app's whole accessibility tree
with it, because deciding which engines to offer reached the Keychain and macOS
put a system dialog in front of the menu. Opening the menu now reads nothing but
values.
[docs/menu-bar.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/menu-bar.md).

## What every model costs, and what is safe to remove

**Settings → Model**, below the engine picker.

The app knew all of this and showed none of it, and the two states that matter
most were invisible: a half-finished download, which looks exactly like nothing,
and the one remaining engine that can transcribe, which looks exactly like the
others.

Every engine now has a row that leads with **what it is for** and keeps the model
name beside it - "The widest language coverage / Whisper", "English and Chinese
in one sentence / SenseVoice-Small", "The most accurate Mandarin characters /
Paraformer-large (zh)" - because the picker has always asked you to choose an
implementation before anything told you what the implementations do. The model
name stays because a licence requires it.

**Readiness is not a checkmark.** A row says Ready, **Incomplete** (bytes on
disk that will not load - an interrupted download leaves exactly this, and
without a name for it the only symptom is a model that downloads itself again on
every launch), Not installed, Downloading *n*%, Preparing, or "No local weights"
for the cloud engine, which is listed rather than hidden so a count of what is on
your disk includes the one engine that puts nothing there.

**Sizes are measured, not advertised.** The cache is walked and its allocated
size reported, because the advertised figure and the real one disagree in both
directions: a CoreML cache is larger than the archive it arrived in, a
half-finished download is smaller, and a cache you deleted in Finder is zero
while the label goes on claiming 240 MB. The totals cover models **and**
recordings, since showing only the models understates the app by the larger half
on a long history.

**Removing is refused rather than disabled**, with a sentence saying why: while a
transcription is running on that engine, while a download is writing into its
directory, when there is nothing there, and for the cloud engine which keeps no
weights. Where a removal is allowed, it names the consequence first - including
what removing the model you are currently dictating with would fall back to,
resolved against what would be *left* and never onto the cloud. Download, Cancel,
Retry, Reveal and Remove are on each row where each is valid.

A recommendation line says which engine this Mac would be best served by and why.
It never downloads, deletes, selects, or proposes the cloud.
[docs/model-inventory.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/model-inventory.md).

## File transcription is findable before you drag

The app has transcribed audio files for a long time - several at once, from a
drop, from Finder's Open With, from the Dock - with a queue that survives a quit.
The only affordance before a drag began was eight words of grey caption, and the
overlay explaining what to do appears once a drag is already in flight, which is
after the moment you needed to know it was possible.

There is now a **visible dashed drop well** above the window's bottom controls,
with an **Open Files…** button beside it for anyone who would never have thought
to drag. Once anything is queued the row becomes a count and a **Show** button
that filters the list to your files, and Show turns into **Show all** - the way
back belongs in the same place you were sent from. The count is answered in the
database rather than over the loaded page, so a queue of thirty files is not
reported as however many happen to be in the first hundred rows.

Nothing about the queue itself changed.
[docs/file-transcription.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/file-transcription.md).

## `echoforge`, a command-line tool

A local control and inspection surface for Kongweh, with eight commands:

```
$ echoforge status
app       0.9.6 (36)  /Applications/EchoForge.app
process   running, pid 4050, since 9/9/26, 11:05 PM
activity  226 recordings, 0 still in flight in the newest 1000
engine    selected whisper, language auto

$ echoforge transcribe ~/Recordings/interview.m4a
So the first thing we should talk about is the migration…

$ echoforge history --query "voice edit" --limit 3
$ echoforge settings
$ echoforge logs --source update-install
$ echoforge update check
```

Every command takes `--help` and `--json`, results go to stdout and everything
else to stderr, so `--json | jq` always gets a document and nothing else.

It is **local-first, like the app**. Five of the eight commands only read;
`start` launches the app, `transcribe` hands it a file, and `update install` can
replace it. There is no server, no daemon, no telemetry, no second copy of the
recordings database - and **no second copy of a speech model**.

That last one is the design decision worth stating: **`transcribe` does not load
a model.** It hands the file to Kongweh exactly as Finder's "Open With" does and
watches the read-only database until the row settles. So your engine, your
personal terms, your Chinese output script and your rewriting style all apply,
and the result is a row in History like any other file you drop. A tool that
loaded its own model would be a second process holding the same 200 MB of weights
and competing for the Neural Engine with the app that is dictating; a tool that
wrote its own rows would be a second writer to a database exactly one thing is
allowed to migrate. This is the only design in which `echoforge transcribe` and a
dragged file cannot produce different text.

Three more things are true by construction rather than by policy. The database is
opened **read-only** and the migrator never runs. **No credential is ever
printed** - the API key lives only in the Keychain and this tool never opens it,
and the row is shown as `<redacted>` rather than omitted, so its absence is
visible. And the update path **adds no security rule and skips none**: the
manifest, the checker and the installer are the app's own code, `--yes` answers
the confirmation and nothing else, and there is no `--force`.

### Installing it

```sh
brew install hsuanchenlin/tap/echoforge
```

Or build it from this repository - it is a target in the app's own Xcode project
and `./run.sh build` produces it, so CI compiles it on every pull request.

The tool is **not** inside `EchoForge.dmg`. The disk image contains the app and
nothing else, and adding a second product to it would change what the release
verifier verifies; the tool is published as its own asset beside the disk image.
[docs/cli.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/cli.md)
is the whole story, including every exit code.

## Everything unchanged from 0.9.5

- **Both new dictation features are off by default**, and neither adds anything
  to what leaves your Mac. Spoken corrections are pure, offline text handling.
  App vocabulary reads one bundle identifier and never reaches the network or the
  cloud provider.
- **Every shortcut is where it was.** ⌥` records, ⌥A opens Ask *and listens*, ⌥S
  asks about the screen, ⌥E edits highlighted text, ⌥M switches engine, ⌥Y runs
  the YouTube command, ⌘F focuses history search - all rebindable in
  **Settings → Shortcuts**. Pausing from the menu bar stands all six down at once
  and is forgotten when you quit.
- **The microphone is still owned by whichever press took it**, so a dictation, an
  Ask, a screen question, a voice edit and now the Setup pane's microphone test
  cannot end one another's recording; a press arriving while something else is
  listening is refused rather than interrupting it.
- **History still says what each entry was** - Dictation, Ask, Voice edit, File
  transcription, YouTube command - and entries from before 0.9.1 are still
  labelled "Older recording" and never relabelled. Search (⌘F) and **Export
  transcript** are unchanged from 0.9.5, still entirely local, and an exported
  file still carries no row id, audio file name or source path.
  [docs/history-search-export.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/history-search-export.md).
- **Chinese transcripts are still written in one script**, Traditional unless you
  picked Simplified, as an output setting only: it never makes recognition
  Chinese, never moves your dictation language and never touches Japanese or
  Korean.
  [docs/chinese-script.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/chinese-script.md).
- **The cloud path is still opt-in and still off.** Two features can be pointed at
  an OpenAI-compatible provider, speech transcription and the spoken
  "Translate to …" command, and switching either takes an explicit acceptance of a
  sheet naming what is uploaded and where. Style rewriting, Fix with AI, voice
  edit (⌥E), the Ask panel (⌥A), screen questions (⌥S), channel-name matching and
  the new app vocabulary have no cloud path at all, enforced in code. The cloud
  engine is also never offered from the menu bar: one tap must not be all it takes
  to start sending dictation to a company. A 0.9.6 install you never point at a
  provider sends nothing, anywhere, with the single exception of the YouTube
  command if you add a channel and use it: that request carries a channel id you
  typed and nothing about you.
  [docs/cloud-api.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/cloud-api.md).
  If you need "not present" rather than "not enabled",
  `Scripts/build_release.sh --offline-only` still builds exactly that; it is not
  published as a separate download here.
- **Kongweh still never checks for updates on its own.** Checking, downloading and
  installing are three separate things you press, and an update is only ever
  downloaded from this repository's own GitHub releases. `echoforge update` runs
  the same code and adds no way around it.
- **The app is still thin.** It ships no model weights. The model you choose is
  fetched on demand and checksummed, SenseVoiceSmall as a verified model pack and
  every other engine by its own downloader, and lands in Application Support,
  where it survives replacing the app. This build does not change the
  SenseVoiceSmall pack, so an engine you have already downloaded is not fetched
  again.
  [docs/model-packs.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/model-packs.md).

## Installing this build

This release is **not signed with an Apple Developer ID and not notarized**, so
macOS will refuse to open it on the first try, typically "the app is damaged and
can't be opened", or "Apple could not verify EchoForge is free of malware".
Nothing is wrong with the download; macOS simply cannot check who built it.

1. Download `EchoForge.dmg`. Its SHA-256 is published beside it as
   `EchoForge.dmg.sha256`, and `shasum -a 256 EchoForge.dmg` is how you check it.
2. Open the disk image and drag `EchoForge.app` into `Applications`.
   Do not run it from the mounted disk image.
3. Then do **one** of the following, once:
   - Right-click (or Control-click) the app in `Applications`, choose **Open**,
     and click **Open** in the dialog; or
   - run `xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/EchoForge.app` in Terminal
     (adjust the path if you installed it elsewhere).

The app in Finder is still `EchoForge.app`; it introduces itself as Kongweh once
it starts.

### Expect to grant permissions again

Because these builds are unsigned, macOS treats each one as a **new
application** for privacy purposes. After you replace your copy, it will ask
again for **Microphone** and **Accessibility**, and for **Screen Recording** the
first time you use ⌥S. **This is expected and is not a regression**; it happens
on every unsigned update and will keep happening until these builds are signed
with a Developer ID.

The new **Settings → Setup** tab is the fastest way to see which of those grants
actually landed, and its microphone test is the fastest way to confirm the input
works before you rely on it.

If a stale **EchoForge** entry is already listed under **System Settings →
Privacy & Security → Accessibility**, remove it with the minus button and add
the new app back; macOS otherwise keeps showing the old entry as granted while
the new build has nothing. The new entry may appear under either name.

The same reason produces one more prompt, and only for people who have stored a
cloud API key: the first time the updated app reads it, macOS asks whether it
may use information stored in your keychain. Choose **Always Allow** and it
stops asking. An install that has never turned the cloud path on never reads the
Keychain at all.

Replacing an existing install keeps your recordings, personal terms, snippets,
styles and downloaded models.

Full instructions, including how to use the Chinese engines and the basics of
recording:
[docs/install.md](https://github.com/hsuanchenlin/EchoForge/blob/master/docs/install.md).

## How this build was made

Produced by `Scripts/build_release.sh` and published only after
`Scripts/verify_release_package.sh` checked every embedded library's signature
and `@rpath` dependency and **started the app**.
